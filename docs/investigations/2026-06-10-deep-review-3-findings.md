PANEL NOTE (competitor-research): The three movers: (1) One-press scan entry via App Intents/ControlWidget, the cheapest way to fix the app's most time-critical moment and the right defense against Visual Intelligence; (2) Geocoded area search, which converts the just-shipped 1.7.0 anchor machinery into a trip-planning capability for nearly free and unlocks the corridor idea later; (3) Offline 'save this area' packs, the category's table-stakes gap and the highest-trust feature for the actual audience, worth anchoring its own minor release. Separately, the App Store 'free, no subscription' copy change is a zero-build, no-review-needed win that should just be done today; it isn't a release feature but addresses the category's loudest complaint. Suggested sequencing for a solo dev: copy now, then scan entry + geocoded search as the next minor (both small), then offline packs as the one after, with the stats card or detour chip as a small companion.

PANEL NOTE (platform-opportunities): Three moves matter most. (1) Visual Intelligence integration is a premise-level match: the system now has a built-in "point your phone at a thing" surface, and Brown Sign is exactly the app that should answer there; it is also free discovery for a no-marketing-budget solo app, and the iOS 26 floor means no deployment-target compromise. (2) LookAroundPreview is the best value-per-line in the list: under a day, gracefully absent in rural areas, and it answers the app's core question ("is this worth pulling off for") right in the detail view. (3) A single "launcher bundle" release: the tab-selection/deep-link refactor plus App Shortcuts, Control Center/Action button control, and Home Screen quick actions, all amortizing the same plumbing that Visual Intelligence and Spotlight also need. The review-prompt fix is not a feature but should ship in the very next build regardless; it is under an hour and the app currently never asks its most engaged Nearby users for a rating.
